Back to Church, Back to Hope

Why the gathered church still matters for weary, stressed-out people — and how to find your way back.

by Pavel Bugriyev, PhD

Online worship was a mercy when doors were closed. But a livestream is not the same as a congregation. Opening the Scriptures, and sharing what his doctoral study found, Pavel Bugriyev invites believers back into the room, where hope is shared, burdens are carried, and no one has to face hardship alone.

Coming soon About the book

What the book offers

God’s Word, a careful study, and a gentle way back

A biblical conviction

Scripture calls God’s people to meet together, stir one another up, and bear one another’s burdens (Heb. 10:24–25; Gal. 6:2). The book shows why that call is a gift for anxious, grieving, and isolated people.

A careful study

Pavel's doctoral research compared U.S. adults who attended church in person, online, or not at all. In-person attenders reported lower post-pandemic stress and more hope, meaning, and social support. The book is honest that this is an association, not a guarantee.

Practical next steps

Stories of illness, addiction, trauma, and church hurt sit alongside a gentle plan for returning, prayers, a thirty-day practice guide, and small-group and leader resources for churches.

“The screen may deliver a sermon. But the gathered church gives us something more: people who know our names, carry our burdens, hear our voices, and help us remember that hope has a body.” — from the Introduction, Back to Church, Back to Hope
